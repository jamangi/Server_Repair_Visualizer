# Dell Server 9712a: an entry-level physical component walk-through

This essay is a study map of component names, locations, appearances, and physical connections in the Dell Server 9712a compute sled. It deliberately postpones component function and service order. It is not a disassembly procedure.

## Orientation and terminology

“Left” and “right” below mean the sled’s own left and right when a technician faces its front. The front is the face with the eight narrow E1.S drive carriers and the network and management ports; the rear is the opposite end, beside the coolant quick disconnects. When the top cover is removed, the three front bays lead to a transverse bank of eight fans, then to the power, cable, cooling, and compute assemblies toward the rear.

The narrow devices across the front are **E1.S solid-state drive (SSD) carriers**, not Secure Digital (SD) card slots. The lesson phrase “SD card slots” is therefore treated here as a reference to the front SSD slots. Dell specifies up to eight E1.S Non-Volatile Memory Express (NVMe) direct drives and identifies the center front assembly as “C Bay (E1.S + I/O).”

Standard acronyms are expanded at first use. Three Dell or NVIDIA labels are intentionally left unexpanded: **PSB**, **IPEX**, and **HMC**. The public Dell manual uses those names but does not define their letters, so inventing expansions would make the lesson less accurate. **C-Link** and **Bianca** are likewise proper component labels rather than acronyms to expand.

The main physical layout and quantities come from Dell’s [Inside the system](https://www.dell.com/support/manuals/en-us/dell-server-9712a/server_9712a/inside-the-system?guid=guid-db45dd2e-26b7-4c9b-84ee-90385df57b15&lang=en-us) figure and [Installation and Service Manual](https://www.dell.com/support/manuals/en-us/dell-server-9712a/server_9712a_ism/Introduction?guid=guid-92f4ae28-dee6-4ba1-96c2-64326abb8d51&lang=en-us). Details explicitly described as “lesson observations” preserve the supplied training notes but still need a second technician or procedure-level check before becoming repair instructions.

## Pass one: from the back toward the front

Begin at the rear of the one-rack-unit (1RU) metal chassis, where the two large **Bianca boards**, or motherboards, fill most of the left and right halves. The lesson calls the left board **Bianca 0** and the right board **Bianca 1**; Dell confirms two Bianca boards but does not use those positional numbers in its public overview. Each is a broad, dark printed-circuit board crowded with copper-colored blocks, connectors, and routed tubing. The boards meet many cable runs from the front bays, and each has a **cold plate**—lesson labels **Cold Plate 0** and **Cold Plate 1**—sitting flat over its major heat-producing package. These cold plates are low, copper-colored rectangular assemblies with red and blue coolant hoses attached. Smaller finned or block-like **heatsinks** sit on several nearby chips and cards; unlike a cable, a heatsink is mechanically clamped to the part beneath it.

At the rear-left service cluster, the lesson places the **HMC module** and **Trusted Platform Module (TPM)** above the left Bianca board. The HMC is a small card held in a metal bracket at the chassis side; Dell’s procedure confirms an HMC module mounted at the Bianca/chassis area and names HMC J21 and J22 connections to the left Bianca. Its HMC cables join the nearby interposer in the lesson narrative. The TPM is a much smaller security module and bracket, secured directly into a slot on a Bianca board by a thumbscrew. Across on the right Bianca sits the **M.2 riser card**: a narrow upright or bracketed card carrying an M.2 SSD. Dell specifically says that this assembly inserts into a slot on the right Bianca, making it a useful left-versus-right landmark.

Along the rear edges are the coolant interfaces. A blue-hosed **left internal manifold** runs down the machine-left side and a red-hosed **right internal manifold** runs down the machine-right side in the observed lesson unit. Each is a long tube-and-block assembly connected by hoses to the two cold plates and ending toward the rear in a **floating quick disconnect (QD)**. The red/blue observation is a strong visual memory aid, while Dell’s public figure confirms the two internal manifolds and their floating QDs without assigning their colors. The hoses arc above portions of the boards, so some connectors and cable channels are partly buried under tubing.

Moving forward beside the Bianca boards brings the power path into view. Two long **12-volt inner busbars**—solid metal power rails rather than flexible wires—run lengthwise with a **busbar partition** between or beside them. A **busbar bridge bracket** is the rigid metal support that spans and holds this area; the lesson’s “busbar bracket” refers to this piece. The flexible **busbar power cable** is visually different: it is a heavy red-and-black cable pair laid flat in a black plastic tray. Dell’s installation instructions explicitly connect that red-and-black busbar cable to the PSB. Together, the busbars, partition, bracket, and cable form an easy-to-recognize cluster of rigid rails, gray/black support hardware, and thick red/black conductors.

Near the middle of the sled, behind the fan bank and offset toward the left/center, is the tray holding the **power distribution board (PDB)** and the **baseboard management controller (BMC) board**. These are compact green or dark printed-circuit boards with several plugs and a few low heatsinks, partly hidden when their tray, fan cover, and cable bundles are installed. The PDB receives power connections including the black-and-yellow PDB J4 cable named by Dell. The BMC sits in the same small tray assembly and has a management Local Area Network (LAN) port exposed at the front through the front I/O arrangement. Nearby, the smaller **interposer board** is a compact connector board under or beside routed harnesses; according to the lesson, both BMC and HMC cables plug into it. That endpoint remains a lesson observation, so the board should be learned as a distinct interposer and not silently merged with the IPEX board.

The middle zone is also where the less visually dominant cabling is easiest to lose. The **C-Link cable** is a named internal data cable routed through this central harness field; “C-Link” should be preserved as printed rather than expanded. Its endpoints are not established in the current public evidence. The **cable-management hardware** is not one signal-carrying component but the set of black channels, holders, clips, ties, and brackets that keeps the Peripheral Component Interconnect Express (**PCIe**) cables, HMC/BMC leads, fan leads, optical runs, power conductors, and hoses below the top-cover line. A useful identification rule is that a cable manager holds or guides other items but has no electrical plug of its own.

Across the full width just ahead of that rear compute-and-power region is the bank of **eight fan modules**. Dell identifies them as eight 40 × 56 mm fans under one fan cover. They appear as a tight row of small black rectangular blower housings, each with a short power/control lead routed toward the boards behind it. Dell explicitly calls out Fan 1 and Fan 2 cables connecting to the left Bianca in its left-bay installation procedure; the remaining fan endpoints should be checked rather than assumed. The fan cover and top fan bracket sit over the row, while cold-plate tubing and internal cables cross or pass close to its rear edge.

Immediately in front of the fan row, the sled divides into three removable bay cages. The **left B bay** contains the NVIDIA **BlueField-3 data processing unit (DPU)**, the **1-gigabit network interface controller (NIC) board**, an **IPEX board**, a PCIe riser/module, and an **Octal Small Form-factor Pluggable (OSFP) card**. The BlueField-3 is a large, low expansion card with a dark printed-circuit board, prominent heatsink, and front-facing management and Quad Small Form-factor Pluggable (QSFP) network openings. A wide PCIe/Ultra-Pass cable path runs from this bay, through the channel between the fans and under the cold-plate tubing, to sockets on the left Bianca. The small 1 Gb NIC board sits near the front edge and connects by a power lead labeled Mezz Board_LAN JP1; its front-facing jack appears as the Host LAN port.

The **IPEX board** is a separate bridge/interface board in that same left bay. It is recognizable by paired black-and-white harnesses held in clips. Dell directs those harnesses to the BlueField DPU and a PCIe auxiliary card, and also names top/bottom IPEX bridge-board cables. The **OSFP card** lies low in the bay with its external OSFP openings at the front. The observed configuration has optical or **fiber-optic cables** associated with the OSFP cards on both sides; externally, optical transceiver/cable assemblies mate with the front OSFP openings. Internally, Dell simply instructs the technician to disconnect the cables attached to each OSFP card, so exact internal endpoints should be traced and labeled from the actual unit rather than guessed.

The **right B bay** is the left bay’s near mirror only at a glance. In the studied PSB configuration it contains the **PSB**, a PCIe riser, and a second OSFP card instead of a second BlueField-3 set. The PSB is a flat PCIe card/enclosure with a large dark board and metal support, inserted into the riser. The lesson’s **PCIe cable** connects into this PSB/riser area, and Dell confirms a removable riser-board cable; Dell also explicitly connects the heavy red-and-black busbar power cable to the PSB. At the front edge, the right OSFP card presents two more rectangular OSFP openings for optical connections. Thus the two outer bays are easiest to distinguish by the left bay’s BlueField/IPEX/NIC cluster versus the right bay’s PSB, even though both carry OSFP hardware.

Finally, reach the center **C bay** at the very front. Eight slim silver/gray **E1.S drive carriers**—the front SSD sockets from the lesson—stand side by side, each with a blue release accent and a direct edge connection behind it. Directly behind the carriers are the lesson’s **Backplane 0** and **Backplane 1**, the two thin E1.S drive-backplane sections into which the drives seat; Dell publicly calls the assembly the Enterprise and Datacenter Standard Form Factor (EDSFF) E1.S backplane but does not confirm the lesson’s 0/1 numbering. The backplanes connect mechanically and electrically to the drives and then to internal power/data harnessing.

Beside the center drive row is the external **front panel input/output (I/O) module**, called the front control panel during the lesson. It is the narrow front-facing bracket carrying the BMC LAN jack, host Universal Serial Bus (USB) port, mini DisplayPort, serial/indicator area, and related openings. Behind it, mounted inside the bracket/cage, is the distinct **control panel board**, or internal control panel: a small flat board connected to the panel card and internal harnessing. Remember the pair as faceplate first and circuit board behind it. At this point the back-to-front walk ends at the handles and front ports of the chassis.

## Pass two: from the front toward the back

Face the sled and start with the eight narrow **E1.S SSD carriers** in the center. These are the “SSD sockets”—not SD memory-card slots—and their repeated silver faces with blue tabs make them the clearest front landmark. Each carrier slides rearward into one of the two lesson-labeled **E1.S Backplane 0 and Backplane 1** sections immediately behind the row. The drive-to-backplane edge connector is the direct connection; the backplane then joins internal power and data cabling. The public Dell terminology is EDSFF E1.S backplane, while the 0/1 split remains a useful but lesson-specific positional label.

Just to the right of the drive row is the **front panel I/O module**, or front control panel. It is a narrow metal-and-board assembly rather than one large motherboard. Its visible openings include the BMC LAN port, host USB port, mini DisplayPort, and serial/indicator controls. Buried immediately behind that face is the separate **control panel board**—the internal control panel—secured inside the I/O bracket and linked to the panel card and the sled’s internal harness. This front module, the drive row, and both backplanes together form the center C bay.

Now scan to the outer bays. On machine-left, the left B bay presents the **BlueField-3 DPU** management port and two QSFP openings above, with two OSFP openings and the **1 Gb NIC/Host LAN** jack below. Inside that cage, the BlueField-3 is the large heatsinked PCIe card; its PCIe or Ultra-Pass cable bundle runs rearward through a narrow channel between the fans and below cold-plate tubing to the left Bianca. The smaller NIC board receives its own JP1 power lead. The nearby **IPEX board** is identified by black-and-white paired harnesses: Dell routes them between the DPU and PCIe auxiliary card and adds IPEX bridge-board cables held by retention clips.

On machine-right, the right B bay’s front face has two OSFP openings. Behind them sits the second **OSFP card**, while the broad internal card is the **PSB** on its PCIe riser. The PSB acronym stays unexpanded because Dell’s manual does not define it. The lesson’s **PCIe cable** attaches in this card/riser region, the riser has its own removable board cable, and the thick red-and-black **busbar power cable** terminates at the PSB. The left and right OSFP cards are low, flat cards with rectangular front apertures; **fiber-optic cables** or optical transceiver assemblies plug into those front OSFP interfaces. Their internal card cables should be traced by connector label before an endpoint is recorded.

Behind all three front bays is the tight crosswise row of **eight 40 × 56 mm fan modules**. Their black square/rectangular frames and repeated spacing make the row look like a wall. Short fan leads route over or around nearby tubing; Dell names Fan 1 and Fan 2 connections to the left Bianca. A fan cover and top bracket hide part of the modules when assembled.

Passing the fan wall, follow the **cable-management system**: low black trays, channels, clips, ties, and holding brackets keep the PCIe and Ultra-Pass bundles, IPEX harnesses, fan leads, HMC/BMC leads, the **C-Link cable**, and thick power conductors organized. The C-Link cable is physically part of this central harness field, but its endpoints are still awaiting validation. The guiding hardware should not be mistaken for a board or cable just because it shares the route.

Immediately behind or beside the fan row is the small tray group containing the **PDB**, expanded as power distribution board, and **BMC**, expanded as baseboard management controller. Look for compact dark/green boards, several plug headers, and low black heatsinks rather than the large copper blocks seen farther back. A black-and-yellow J4 power lead connects to the PDB. The BMC is associated with the front BMC LAN connection through the internal I/O path. Close to this tray is the small **interposer board**; in the lesson map, BMC and HMC cables meet it. Keep that provisional connection separate from the IPEX board and confirm the printed connector labels during hands-on study.

The power hardware becomes larger and more structural as the walk continues. The **12 V inner busbars** are rigid longitudinal metal rails with a **busbar partition** between/alongside them. Above or across this zone is the **busbar bridge bracket**, a rigid metal support. In its black plastic channel lies the flexible red-and-black **busbar power cable**, running between the power-distribution area and the PSB. These four names—busbar, partition, bridge bracket, and cable—describe visibly different pieces and should not be collapsed into one “power bar.”

Along the two chassis sides, the coolant path is equally easy to follow by color in the lesson unit: the **blue left manifold** and **red right manifold** are long tubular assemblies seated in leak trays. Hoses rise from them and curve over the two **cold plates**, while their rear ends terminate at floating quick disconnects. Cold Plate 0 on the left Bianca and Cold Plate 1 on the right Bianca are broad copper-colored contact assemblies. Nearby **heatsinks** are smaller finned or block-shaped metal pieces sitting directly atop chips and cards; they have no hose unless the observed part is actually a cold plate.

At the rear, the machine opens into the two broad **Bianca motherboards**: lesson-numbered Bianca 0 on machine-left and Bianca 1 on machine-right. Their dark board surfaces, dense connector rows, copper-colored thermal hardware, and crossing red/blue hoses make them visually dominant. The front-bay Ultra-Pass/PCIe cables and other board harnesses arrive here after passing the fan channel. The rear-wall bracket forms a metal boundary/support at their back edge.

Use the small rear cards to finish the identification walk. On the left-rear lesson cluster, the bracketed **HMC module** connects by HMC J21/J22 leads to the left Bianca, and its lesson-mapped HMC cable continues to the interposer. Nearby, the very small **TPM**, or Trusted Platform Module, sits in a Bianca slot and is held by a bracket and thumbscrew. On the opposite motherboard, the **M.2 riser card** is an upright bracketed assembly plugged into the right Bianca; a narrow M.2 **solid-state drive** lies on it and is held by a single screw. At the far rear, the blue and red manifold hoses finish at their floating coolant quick disconnects. That closes the front-to-back pass with every major entry-level component connected to a physical landmark.

## Compact connection recap

- E1.S SSD carriers plug into the E1.S backplane sections; the backplanes join internal power/data harnesses.
- The front panel I/O module contains the visible ports; the control panel board sits behind it and connects to the internal panel/harness.
- The left BlueField-3/IPEX/NIC bay sends PCIe/Ultra-Pass and board harnesses rearward toward the left Bianca; black-and-white IPEX harnesses connect the DPU and PCIe auxiliary card.
- Both OSFP cards accept external optical transceiver or fiber-optic cable assemblies and also have internal card cables whose exact endpoints must be traced.
- The right PSB sits in a PCIe riser, receives the lesson-identified PCIe/riser connection, and is explicitly connected to the red-and-black busbar power cable.
- Fan leads run rearward; Dell explicitly identifies Fan 1 and Fan 2 connections to the left Bianca.
- The PDB receives a black-and-yellow J4 power cable. The BMC, HMC, interposer, and C-Link relationships beyond the verified labels remain lesson observations pending endpoint confirmation.
- The blue left and red right manifolds connect by hoses to the two cold plates and finish at rear floating quick disconnects.
- The M.2 riser plugs into the right Bianca; the TPM plugs into a Bianca slot; the HMC is bracketed at the left Bianca/chassis region and connects to the left Bianca through HMC-labeled leads.
